Attendance

6 deviations
Daily Attendance Less Than 60%
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkOut of all students enrolled in a section, how many came to at least one class that day.
Flagged whenFewer than 60 out of every 100 students turned up. Example: a section of 100 students where only 52 attended.
Why it mattersMore than a third of the section missed the day's teaching and will fall behind.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (Attendance %). Example: NIAT Chevella Batch-1 S-001 (45%)
Consecutive Attendance Below 60% in Last 3 Days
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkThe same daily attendance figure, looked at across the last 3 days of that section.
Flagged whenAll 3 days in a row were below 60%.
Why it mattersThis is not a one-day dip. Something is keeping students away and needs a campus visit, not just a reminder.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (Attendance %). Example: S-VYASA Batch-1 S-002 (52%)
Zero Attendance Marked for a Scheduled Session
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkEach scheduled class slot, and whether even one student was marked present in it.
Flagged whenNot a single student was marked present for that slot.
Why it mattersAlmost always the instructor forgetting to mark attendance. Until corrected, that section's numbers are wrong.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name (Number of zero-attendance sessions). Example: ANNAMACHARYA Batch-1 (3)
Significant Drop Between Consecutive Sessions
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkHow many students were present in each class slot, compared with the slot just before it on the same day.
Flagged whenMore than 10 students disappeared between two back-to-back slots.
Why it mattersStudents are walking out mid-day, or attendance is being marked for only part of the day.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name (Number of sessions with significant drop). Example: NIAT-KKH Batch-1 (2)
Student Continuously Absent Beyond 7-Days
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkEach student's day-by-day attendance, to find unbroken stretches of absence.
Flagged whenA student has been absent 7 or more days in a row with no attendance in between.
Why it mattersStudents absent this long usually stop coming back. Highest priority for a call to the student and parent.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name (Number of students absent 7+ days continuously). Example: S-VYASA Batch-1 (109)
Instructor Attendance Not Marked Within 10 Minutes of Session Start
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkThe gap between a class's scheduled start time and the instructor actually starting it.
Flagged whenThe instructor started more than 10 minutes after the scheduled time.
Why it mattersStudents were sitting and waiting, and the class lost that much teaching time.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (Number of sessions where attendance was late). Example: S-VYASA Batch-1 S-003 (4)

Instructor Platform

8 deviations
Session Completion Rate Below 90%
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkOut of all classes scheduled at a campus for the day, how many reached completed status.
Flagged whenFewer than 90 out of every 100 were completed.
Why it mattersEither the classes did not happen, or they happened and nobody marked them. Both need checking with the campus.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Session completion %). Example: MALLA REDDY (78%)
TeachOS Portal Recording Adoption Below 60%
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkOut of today's classes at a campus, how many were recorded through the TeachOS portal.
Flagged whenFewer than 60 out of every 100 classes used the portal.
Why it mattersNo portal recording means no QA review, no punctuality data and no way to verify what was actually taught.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Portal recording adoption %). Example: ANNAMACHARYA (45%)
Company Mic Usage Below 90%
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkOut of the classes taken at a campus, how many used the company-issued mic.
Flagged whenFewer than 90 out of every 100 classes used it.
Why it mattersWithout the company mic, recording audio is poor so the quality score for that class cannot be trusted.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Mic usage %). Example: S-VYASA (72%)
Average Pre-Lecture Readiness Score Below 70%
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkThe checklist instructors fill before starting a class, and how much of it they completed on average across the campus.
Flagged whenLess than 70% of the checklist was completed on average.
Why it mattersClasses are starting without the projector, content or lab setup confirmed.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Average readiness score %). Example: NIAT Chevella (58%)
Average QA Score Below 6.5
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkThe quality score given to each class at a campus, averaged across the day.
Flagged whenThe campus average falls below 6.5 out of 10.
Why it mattersTeaching quality was poor across the campus that day. Open individual class reports to see what pulled the average down.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Average QA score out of 10). Example: SGU (5.8)
QA Report Open Rate Below 80%
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkOut of the QA reports generated at a campus, how many were actually opened and read.
Flagged whenFewer than 80 out of every 100 reports were opened.
Why it mattersThe feedback exists but nobody reads it, so the same teaching mistakes repeat class after class.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (QA report open rate %). Example: NIAT-KKH (62%)
QA Report Generated Beyond 2-Hour Window
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkThe gap between a class ending and its QA report being created.
Flagged whenThe report took more than 2 hours to appear after the class ended.
Why it mattersBy the time feedback arrives the instructor has already taken more classes with the same mistakes uncorrected.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (Number of reports delayed beyond 2 hours). Example: TAKSHASILA Batch-1 S-002 (3)
Malpractice or Suspicious Recording Flagged
VPVamsi/Prudhvi
What we checkClass recordings, for any malpractice or tampering signal raised by the system.
Flagged whenThe system raises such a signal against a recording.
Why it mattersSomeone needs to watch the recording and confirm what happened. This cannot be judged from the data alone.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (Number of flagged recordings). Example: ANNAMACHARYA Batch-1 S-001 (1)

Faculty

3 deviations
Instructor No-Show — Session Missed Entirely
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkClasses whose time is over, and whether the instructor started the class or its evaluation at any point.
Flagged whenThe class time has passed with no sign the instructor ever started it.
Why it mattersThe class was missed completely. Students lost that slot and it needs to be rescheduled.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (Number of missed sessions). Example: NIAT Chevella Batch-1 S-001 (2)
Instructor Late More than 3 Sessions in Last 7 Days
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkFor each instructor, how many classes in the last 7 days started more than 15 minutes after the scheduled time.
Flagged whenMore than 3 of their classes started late.
Why it mattersThis is a repeated habit, not a one-off. Every late start cuts teaching time and students are left waiting.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Number of late sessions in last 7 days). Example: SGU (5)
Instructor Extended Sessions Beyond Allowed Limit
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkFor each instructor, how many classes in the last 7 days ran more than 5 minutes past the scheduled end time.
Flagged whenMore than 10 out of every 100 of their classes overran.
Why it mattersOverruns push the following class late, so one instructor running over makes others look unpunctual.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (% sessions that overran). Example: TAKSHASILA (22%)

Scheduling

3 deviations
Completed Session with No QA Report
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkEvery class marked as completed, and whether a QA report was created for it.
Flagged whenThe class is marked completed but no QA report exists.
Why it mattersWe have no record of what was taught or how well. It cannot be reviewed, scored or improved.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name (Number of completed sessions without QA report). Example: SGU Batch-1 (5)
Session Not Marked Completed
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkClasses whose scheduled end time has already passed, and their status on the instructor portal.
Flagged whenThe class time is over but the status is still pending, started or not marked.
Why it mattersEvery report will count it as not delivered until the instructor marks it.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name (Number of sessions not marked completed). Example: NIAT-KKH Batch-1 (7)
More than 20% Daily Scheduled Sessions Cancelled
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkOut of all classes scheduled at a campus for the day, how many were cancelled.
Flagged whenMore than 20 out of every 100 were cancelled. Example: 30 classes planned, 8 cancelled.
Why it mattersA large part of the day's teaching did not happen and has to be made up later in the semester.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name (Cancellation %). Example: ANNAMACHARYA Batch-1 (35%)

Assessments

2 deviations
Exam Participation Less Than 60%
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkOut of all students in a section, how many actually appeared for the exam.
Flagged whenFewer than 60 out of every 100 appeared. Example: 80 students in the section, only 40 wrote the exam.
Why it mattersThe section's results don't represent the section, and students who missed have no score on record.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (Participation %). Example: NIAT Chevella Batch-1 S-003 (48%)
More than 20% of Students Scored Zero
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkOut of all students in a section, how many scored zero in the exam.
Flagged when20 or more out of every 100 students scored zero.
Why it mattersThat many zeros is rarely about student ability. Usually the question paper or platform failed. Check this before treating it as a performance problem.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (% students scored zero). Example: MALLA REDDY Batch-1 S-001 (25%)

LMS / Portal

1 deviation
Scheduled Units Not Accessed In 24 Hours
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkThe learning portal content linked to a class, and whether students in that section opened it after the class was taken.
Flagged when24 hours have passed since the class and progress on that content is still zero.
Why it mattersThe class happened but nobody practised on it afterwards, so the concept is unlikely to have stuck.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Batch Name + Section Name (% students who did not access). Example: TAKSHASILA Batch-1 S-002 (80%)

Student Support

3 deviations
More than 5 Tickets Raised of Same Category
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkSupport tickets raised by students at a campus in one day, grouped by the type of issue.
Flagged whenMore than 5 tickets of the same type come from one campus on the same day.
Why it mattersMany students complaining about the same thing points to one campus problem to fix, not six separate complaints to answer.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Number of tickets of same category). Example: NIAT Chevella (8)
Student Raised 3+ Tickets on Same Issue in 3 Days
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkEach student's tickets on the same type of issue over a 3-day window.
Flagged whenThe student raised tickets on 3 different days and 3 or more tickets in total.
Why it mattersThe student's problem is not getting solved and they keep coming back. Someone needs to own it and close it properly.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Number of students with repeat tickets). Example: S-VYASA (4)
Ticket Open Beyond 48-Hour SLA
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkHow long each unresolved student ticket has been open since it was raised.
Flagged whenThe ticket has been open more than 48 hours and is still not resolved.
Why it matters48 hours is the resolution time we promise students. Every ticket listed here is already past that promise.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Number of tickets breaching 48-hr SLA). Example: MALLA REDDY (6)

Parent Comms

3 deviations
Attendance Notification Not Triggered for Absent Students
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkStudents marked absent today whose parent account is linked, and whether the absence notification actually reached that parent.
Flagged whenThe student was absent but no notification went out to the parent.
Why it mattersParents expect to be told the same day their child misses class. When this fails silently, they find out much later and lose trust.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Number of students whose parents were not notified). Example: SGU (12)
More than 10 Parent Tickets Raised in a Single Day
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkTickets raised by parents at a campus in a single day.
Flagged when10 or more parent tickets come from one campus on one day.
Why it mattersA spike like this usually follows an incident at the campus or a communication gap that left parents in the dark.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Number of parent tickets raised). Example: ANNAMACHARYA (14)
More than 20% Students Without Parent Account Linked
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkOut of all students at a campus, how many have no parent account linked to them.
Flagged whenMore than 20 out of every 100 students have no parent linked.
Why it mattersThose parents cannot receive attendance alerts, announcements or any update about their child.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (% students without parent linked). Example: NIAT-KKH (28%)

Why it mattersParents and students see announcements appear and then vanish, so they stop taking the channel seriously.

Reporting

1 deviation
Syllabus Completion Below 80% of Expected Progress
KMKarthik/Manoj
What we checkThe classes planned for a campus up to the current week of the semester, against how many were actually completed.
Flagged whenFewer than 80 out of every 100 planned classes were completed.
Why it mattersThe campus is behind the syllabus plan, and the gap keeps growing every week unless extra classes are added.
📋 Critical Cases formatEach entry shows: Campus Name (Syllabus completion %). Example: MALLA REDDY (65%)
